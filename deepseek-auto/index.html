02 The case against the feed

You already have a device that wants you. This one doesn’t.

Every screen you own is a slot machine with a camera. TOCK is a small plastic object that plays a game and then stops. It has no browser. It cannot tell you about anything. If something happens in the world, you’ll find out later — from a person.

It weighs 118 grams, which is the weight of a pocket notebook and not the weight of a decision. It charges over USB-C. It has a headphone jack, because it was designed by people who remember why.

“No accounts. No updates. No dark patterns, no light patterns.”

  • 3.5 mm jack

Note 01

It closes. That’s a feature.

The lid is a physical do-not-disturb. Snapping it shut stops the game, the clock, and the conversation.

Note 02

8 megabytes. That’s a whole game.

Carts hold the entire game, offline, forever. No downloads, no day-one patch, no ceremony.

Note 03

14 hours, no charger anxiety.

That’s a flight to Lisbon and back, mostly asleep. Standby is 60 days, for people who forget things on purpose.

03 Two screens, one folded lid

A hinge, not a highlight reel.

The top screen plays. The bottom one counts, maps, ticks, or shuts up. Both are 400×320, both are visible at once, and neither one slides out of a pocket to sell you something.

The lid has two detents and no software. You will open it about four times a day, and each time it will make the small, satisfying sound of a thing that was designed to be opened.

“It closes. That’s a feature.”

Questions we actually get

Is it a phone?

No.

Can I check my email on it?

No. That’s the whole thing. There is no network stack in the firmware, so there is nothing to hack, jailbreak, or convince.

What happens if I lose a cartridge?

The game is $18. The console still works. You will be annoyed for an afternoon, which is the correct amount of annoyed.
